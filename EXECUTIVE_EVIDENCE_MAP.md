# John Cota — Executive Evidence Map (V1)

This is the claim-control source for the V1 website. It separates what the artifacts prove from John’s recollection and from claims that still need validation. Original evidence remains read-only.

## Positioning architecture

**Executive thesis:** John Cota is an executive transformation leader rooted in brand and creative. He diagnoses ambiguous cross-functional problems and builds the teams, systems, governance, partnerships and technology-enabled operating models that make change durable.

**Primary audience:** executive recruiters, CEOs, CMOs and hiring leaders seeking transformation leadership with genuine creative and brand depth.

**Pillars:**

1. Brand is enterprise infrastructure, not surface expression.
2. Creative operations can become an organizational operating system.
3. Partnerships work when strategy, identity, governance and execution align.
4. Measurement and financial discipline belong inside creative leadership.
5. Automation and AI are the next expression of a longstanding systems mindset.

## Metric guardrails

- Never use the existing-site claim that market presence grew from 15% to 70% until its definition and source are validated.
- Never describe the 42% figure as a reduction. The validated source describes a 42% year-over-year increase in connected-TV completion rate.
- CRC campaign figures in the CEO update are documented, but need careful attribution before publication as personal impact.
- Planned objectives, authored strategies and completed outcomes are three different evidence categories.
- “Led” is used only where role records or authored materials support leadership responsibility; otherwise prefer “helped advance,” “directed within,” or “the team delivered.”

## Case-study template for post-V1 expansion

1. Organizational condition — what was true before the work.
2. The real problem — the cross-functional constraint beneath the brief.
3. John’s mandate — precise role and decision authority.
4. Intervention — system, team, brand, partnership and/or technology built.
5. Adoption — how people, governance and behaviors changed.
6. Evidence — primary artifacts and substantiated metrics.
7. Outcome — documented result, clearly separated from recollection.
8. What endured — capability left behind.

## Visual direction

**Concept: The Transformation Schematic.** An executive editorial system with the precision of an operating diagram: disciplined grid, visible rules and evidence labels, interrupted by one expressive signal—the oversized Newsreader italic in the hero. The aesthetic connects creative judgment with systems thinking.

- Ink `#15191C`, paper `#F3F5F4`, white `#FFFFFF`, signal blue `#1647FF`, process ice `#DCEcff`, intervention lime `#D6FF43`.
- Manrope for direct executive clarity; Newsreader for human judgment and change; DM Mono for evidence, systems and labels.
- Avoid stock imagery in V1. The work is presented as operating architecture, reducing dependence on uneven archival assets and avoiding a conventional portfolio feel.
